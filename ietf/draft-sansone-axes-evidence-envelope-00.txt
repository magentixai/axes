    AXES: A Recomputable Evidence Envelope for Autonomous Execution
                draft-sansone-axes-evidence-envelope-00

Abstract

   This document describes AXES, a public working draft for recording
   evidence of autonomous execution, that is, actions taken by software
   agents under delegated authority.  It specifies the parts of AXES
   that are settled: a canonical byte form and hash scope for the
   evidence record (the Standards Envelope), a hash chain over a
   declared stream, a block for external anchoring of digests with
   earned rather than asserted strength, a four-state result vocabulary
   for verification, and requirement profiles that a relying party
   applies to evidence to produce a deterministic evaluation record.
   The byte rules, the hash chain and the release anchors have been
   reproduced independently from the text; profile evaluation has a
   reference implementation.  The design keeps evidence, interpretation
   and enforcement apart, and requires that the absence of evidence be
   reported as absence rather than read as a pass.  AXES is offered as
   one input format to format-neutral audit readers, not as a
   replacement for other record formats.  Parts of AXES that are not yet
   settled, including the full envelope schema and the exact
   construction for redaction-tolerant hashing, are named as open
   issues.  The chosen direction for redaction, salted per-field hash
   commitments following the approach of SD-JWT with an encoding
   specific to AXES, is recorded.

1.  Introduction

   Software agents increasingly act on delegated authority: they move
   money, change records and commit organisations to obligations.  When
   one of those actions is later questioned, by an auditor, a regulator,
   a counterparty or a court, the question is rarely "what did the logs
   say".  It is "what can be shown, by whom, from which records, and
   with what limits".  Logs serve engineers debugging software.
   Evidence serves the people who carry the consequences.

   AXES (Autonomous eXecution Evidence Standard) [AXES-REPO] is a public
   working draft that defines an evidence record for that purpose, the
   Standards Envelope (SE), and the rules by which a third party can
   recompute, anchor and evaluate it without access to the system that
   produced it.  Its governing design rule is that a competent third
   party should be able to produce a credible, structured account of
   what happened from the open specification and the evidence alone.

   AXES is deliberately not a logging format, a policy or blocking
   engine, an identity system, a payment rail, or a compliance
   certificate.  It evidences; it does not enforce and it does not
   certify.

   AXES is written from the position of the party who must later rely on
   a record, not the party that produces it.  It is not specific to
   payments.  It is meant for machine-to-machine actions of every kind,
   including payments, data exchange, compute, identity assertions,
   supply-chain actions and industrial control; the published corpus
   contains an industrial trace alongside a financial one.  An AXES
   record sits beside the protocol that carries an action, as a separate
   evidence record, not inside it: the protocol's own messages are



Sansone                    Expires 5 May 2027                   [Page 3]

Internet-Draft           AXES Evidence Envelope            November 2026


   referenced by digest, exactly as that protocol defines them.  AXES
   records the facts a relying party needs to verify later, such as
   which authority an action relied on and that authority's state at the
   time of the action.  It references the processes that produced those
   facts (billing schedules, organisational workflows) rather than
   modelling them.

1.1.  Scope of this document

   This -00 is scoped to the parts of AXES that have a published
   reference implementation and pinned two-sided test vectors.  The byte
   rules and the hash chain have also been reproduced independently from
   the specification text; profile evaluation so far has implementations
   by the authors only (see Section 11):

   *  the canonical byte form, hash scope and hash chain (Section 4 and
      Section 5);

   *  the declared capture boundary and the treatment of absence
      (Section 6);

   *  the external anchoring block and its verification rules
      (Section 7);

   *  the four-state verification result vocabulary (Section 8);

   *  requirement profiles and evaluation records (Section 9);

   *  the conformance-vector method (Section 10).

   The full field catalogue of the envelope (sixteen planned modules, of
   which two are published for challenge), the envelope JSON Schema, the
   extension model, the envelope signature profile and the construction
   for redaction-tolerant hashing are not specified here.  They are
   listed in Section 15; the chosen direction for redaction is recorded
   in Section 5.1.  Field names used in examples follow the published
   corpus of record [AXES-GT21]; they are not frozen by this document.

1.2.  Position among the agent audit drafts

   Several individual drafts in the agent audit space define record
   formats ([I-D.gilda-audit-agent-audit-record]), audit architectures
   ([I-D.kuehlewind-audit-architecture]) and evidence requirements
   ([I-D.sergeev-audit-task-requirements]).  AXES does not compete to be
   the single record format.  It takes the position that three roles
   should stay separate:

   *  a record format, of which AXES is one;



Sansone                    Expires 5 May 2027                   [Page 4]

Internet-Draft           AXES Evidence Envelope            November 2026


   *  an enforcement point, which decides whether an action may proceed
      and which AXES never is;

   *  a format-neutral reader, which takes records in any declared
      format and returns a bounded finding.

   AXES contributes to that reader the rules it needs from any format:
   recomputable digests, a declared hash scope, honest reporting of
   absence, anchors whose strength is earned, and results that never
   collapse "could not check" into "checked and found nothing".  The
   verification result vocabulary is taken from
   [I-D.krausz-verification-state].  Action identity converges with
   [I-D.etcheverry-action-ref], which uses the same RFC 8785 canonical
   form.  Section 12 gives the details.

1.4.  Terminology

   Standards Envelope (SE):  One AXES evidence record: an atomic,
      append-only record of one execution event, serialised as a JSON
      object.

   Emitter:  The software that produces envelopes at the point of
      execution.

   Stream:  An ordered sequence of envelopes from one emitter, linked by
      a hash chain (Section 4.4).

   Subject:  The thing a digest, anchor or evaluation is about: an
      envelope, a chain head, a bundle manifest or a release statement.

   Capture boundary:  The declared scope within which the emitter
      commits to producing an envelope for every action of a declared
      kind.

   Commit boundary:  The point at which an action stops being advisory
      and has external consequence (for example, a payment instruction
      is released).

   Anchor:  A commitment of a subject digest to a mechanism outside the




Sansone                    Expires 5 May 2027                   [Page 5]

Internet-Draft           AXES Evidence Envelope            November 2026


      emitter's control (a timestamp authority, a public chain, a
      transparency log) that a third party can check offline.

   Relying party:  A party that uses evidence to reach a conclusion, for
      example an auditor, a regulator, an insurer or a counterparty.

   Requirement profile:  A declared, versioned and immutable statement
      of what a relying party requires of a subject.

   Evaluation record:  The deterministic result of applying a
      requirement profile to a subject.

   Reader:  Software that verifies and evaluates records and returns a
      bounded finding.  A reader is neither an emitter nor an
      enforcement point.

   Release statement:  A small JSON document, published with a release
      of a corpus, that lists each bundle's chain head and bundle
      manifest hash.  The SHA-256 of its bytes is what the release's
      external anchors commit to (Section 11).

2.  Design Principles

   The following principles constrain every part of AXES.  They are
   stated here because the mechanisms in later sections follow from
   them.

   Append-only:  Envelopes are never edited.  A correction is a new
      envelope that names the one it supersedes and gives a reason.

   Pointers and hashes only:  Envelopes do not carry raw payloads,
      documents, screenshots or secrets.  Artefacts are referenced by
      URI and digest, with declared classification and retention.

   Fact separated from interpretation:  Every value carries its






Sansone                    Expires 5 May 2027                   [Page 7]

Internet-Draft           AXES Evidence Envelope            November 2026


      provenance: where it came from (evidence_origin), on what basis it
      is asserted (assertion_basis) and how far it is corroborated
      (corroboration_state).  Judgements such as trust, intent or
      sufficiency are not stored in the envelope.

   Trust is computed, never stored:  An envelope never carries a trust,
      confidence or sufficiency value about itself.  Sufficiency is the
      output of a relying party's requirement profile applied to the
      evidence (Section 9).

   Gaps are disclosed, never hidden:  Completeness is measured against a
      declared population.  The absence of evidence is reported as
      absence, with its reason where one is known (Section 6).

   Strength is earned:  A strong claim such as "externally anchored" is
      true only when a verifier can confirm it from the evidence
      (Section 7.4).  A simulated or self-held mechanism never earns it.

   Scoped assurance language only:  No element or result implies
      "compliant", "safe" or "guaranteed".  Results carry a basis and
      the limits of what was checked.

   Vendor and runtime neutrality:  Conformance is defined by the public
      specification, reference verifier and public test vectors, never
      by any product's ingestion behaviour.

3.  The Standards Envelope

   This section is informative.  It describes the envelope so that the
   normative sections that follow have a referent.  The field catalogue
   is published for challenge in [AXES-REPO] and is not frozen.

   An envelope is a JSON object.  In the published corpus of record
   [AXES-GT21] every envelope carries, among others:

   *  identity and kind: se_version, envelope_id, event_kind;

   *  time: occurred_at (when the event happened), emitted_at (when the
      emitter produced the record) and recorded_at (when it was
      persisted), together with timestamp_source and a clock-
      synchronisation confidence;

   *  placement: organisation, tenant and environment identifiers,
      trace_id, span_id, and a monotonic sequence number within the
      stream;

   *  the action and its authority: actor, authority, operation,
      execution_phase and execution_mode;



Sansone                    Expires 5 May 2027                   [Page 8]

Internet-Draft           AXES Evidence Envelope            November 2026


   *  epistemic status: evidence_quality (evidence_origin,
      assertion_basis, corroboration_state);

   *  privacy: classification and the pseudonymous references used for
      people;

   *  integrity: the canonicalisation identifier, hash algorithm,
      previous envelope hash, signing key identity, envelope hash and
      envelope signature.

   Envelopes can carry a namespaced extensions object, which lies inside
   the hash scope like every other member (Section 4).

   Every numeric quantity inside the hash scope is exact (Section 4.2).
   Asset-denominated quantities use an Amount object: value is a string
   of base-10 integer atomic units, decimals is the declared scale, and
   asset is a namespaced string (iso4217:<code> [ISO4217] for fiat,
   caip19:<asset id> [CAIP-19] for crypto-assets).  Ratios that must be
   recorded use exact rationals (numerator, denominator, both integer
   strings).  Derived ratios, such as how much of a delegated limit was
   used, are not stored; they are computed from their operands when a
   report is produced.

4.  Canonical Form and Hashing

4.1.  Canonical byte form

   The canonical byte form of an AXES JSON object is the output of the
   JSON Canonicalization Scheme [RFC8785].  The input MUST be I-JSON
   [RFC7493]; in particular, an object with duplicate member names MUST
   be rejected rather than canonicalised.

   The identifier RFC8785-JCS, carried in
   integrity.canonicalization_version, denotes exactly the following,
   and nothing else:

   *  the RFC 8785 JSON Canonicalization Scheme at its published
      revision;

   *  output as UTF-8 bytes;

   *  object members sorted by UTF-16 code units (RFC 8785
      Section 3.2.3), not by code point and not by any locale collation;

   *  no Unicode normalisation (RFC 8785 Section 3.1), so the NFC and
      NFD forms of the same visible key are distinct members;





Sansone                    Expires 5 May 2027                   [Page 9]

Internet-Draft           AXES Evidence Envelope            November 2026


   *  the digest algorithm is declared separately
      (integrity.hash_algorithm) and is not implied by this identifier.

   A second implementer MUST be able to reproduce the canonical bytes
   from this statement alone.  A change to any property above defines a
   new identifier; RFC8785-JCS is never redefined.  Independently of the
   identifier, every digest in AXES is written as bare lowercase
   hexadecimal with no algorithm prefix.

4.2.  Value domain

   Inside the hash scope:

   *  a JSON number with a fractional part, or a non-finite value, MUST
      NOT appear;

   *  a JSON integer, such as sequence_number, MUST lie within -(2^53 -
      1) to 2^53 - 1, which every RFC 8785 implementation represents
      exactly; a larger integer is carried as a base-10 string;

   *  timestamps are strings in UTC under RFC 3339 [RFC3339], with
      exactly three fractional digits and the suffix Z (for example
      2026-06-09T09:01:00.400Z);

   *  an extensions object MUST NOT override any member defined by AXES.

   A verifier that meets a fractional, non-finite or out-of-range number
   inside the hash scope, or input that is not I-JSON, reports
   contradicted (value_outside_domain); it does not round, convert or
   canonicalise the value.

4.3.  Envelope hash

   The envelope hash is computed as follows:

   1.  Remove integrity.envelope_hash and integrity.envelope_signature
       from the envelope.

   2.  Canonicalise the remaining object under the identifier in
       integrity.canonicalization_version.

   3.  Apply the algorithm named in integrity.hash_algorithm to the
       canonical bytes.

   4.  Encode the digest as bare lowercase hexadecimal.






Sansone                    Expires 5 May 2027                  [Page 10]

Internet-Draft           AXES Evidence Envelope            November 2026


   Every other member of the envelope is inside the hash scope,
   including envelope_id, recorded_at, integrity.signing_key_id and
   integrity.previous_envelope_hash.

   The digest algorithm is agile.  The published corpus uses SHA-256
   [FIPS180-4].  An implementation MAY use another algorithm over the
   same canonical bytes, provided that integrity.hash_algorithm names
   it.  A verifier that does not implement the named algorithm, or the
   named canonicalisation identifier, MUST report the check as not
   evaluated, reason instrument_failure, condition
   hash_algorithm_unsupported (Section 8), never as verified or
   contradicted, and MUST NOT substitute another algorithm.  The same
   applies when either member is absent: a verifier does not assume
   SHA-256 or RFC 8785.

4.4.  Hash chain

   Each envelope in a stream carries integrity.previous_envelope_hash,
   the envelope hash of the envelope immediately before it in the
   stream.  The first envelope in a stream carries a string of the
   character "0" as long as a hexadecimal digest of the stream's
   algorithm: sixty-four zeros for SHA-256.  Every envelope in a stream
   MUST use the same integrity.hash_algorithm.  Moving a stream to
   another algorithm is not specified (Section 15).  The first envelope
   has sequence number 1, and each later envelope's sequence number is
   one more than its predecessor's.  The chain head is the envelope hash
   of the last envelope in the stream.  This document does not define a
   stream identifier; in the corpus each bundle holds one stream.

   A verifier recomputes every envelope hash, checks that each
   previous_envelope_hash equals the recomputed hash of its predecessor,
   and checks that sequence numbers within the stream are contiguous.  A
   recomputed hash that differs from the stored integrity.envelope_hash
   is a contradiction (envelope_hash_mismatch).  A broken link is a
   contradiction (chain_break).  A gap in the sequence numbers is
   indeterminate (sequence_gap): something is missing, but the chain
   alone cannot say why.

4.5.  Bundle manifest digest

   A bundle is a set of files: the envelope stream plus the artefacts
   its envelopes reference.  Its manifest maps each file path, using
   forward slashes and relative to the bundle root, to the lowercase
   hexadecimal SHA-256 of that file's bytes.  The manifest file itself
   is excluded.  The bundle manifest hash is the SHA-256 of the RFC 8785
   bytes of that mapping, a single JSON object whose member names are
   the paths and whose values are the file digests.  File digests are
   over the file bytes exactly as stored, with no canonicalisation.  A



Sansone                    Expires 5 May 2027                  [Page 11]

Internet-Draft           AXES Evidence Envelope            November 2026


   verifier can check a bundle by recomputing every file digest and the
   manifest hash, without running any software supplied by the emitter.
   It resolves every path inside the bundle and does not read a path
   that is absolute, contains .. or a backslash, or leads outside the
   bundle through a link (bundle_path_unsafe, contradicted).  A changed
   file is contradicted (bundle_file_hash_mismatch), a listed file that
   is not present is indeterminate (bundle_file_absent), and a
   recomputed manifest hash that differs from the stated or pinned one
   is contradicted (bundle_manifest_hash_mismatch).

5.  Hash Scope Discipline

   A digest whose scope is narrower than what a reader consumes leaves a
   region that can be changed while the record still verifies.  AXES
   therefore treats the hash scope as a declared, reviewable part of the
   record.  This section states the current AXES position, which is
   published for challenge.  In the published corpus the hash scope is
   fixed by Section 4 and no per-envelope declaration is carried.  The
   rules below govern any declaration or exclusion added later.

   1.  Scope defaults to inclusion.  Every member is inside the hash
       unless an exclusion is declared.

   2.  Every exclusion MUST name a reason from a closed vocabulary
       (hash_scope_exclusion_reason).  The two exclusions in force,
       integrity.envelope_hash and integrity.envelope_signature, have
       the reason self_referential.

   3.  Sensitivity of a value MUST NOT be used as a reason to exclude it
       from the hash scope.  Until the redaction construction is
       specified (Section 5.1), a sensitive value is kept out of the
       envelope and referenced by digest; once it is, such a value will
       be committed at emission and disclosed selectively.

   4.  A declaration of the hash scope, when carried, MUST be inside the
       hash scope it declares.  Otherwise an attacker could edit the
       declaration to exclude a field, change that field, and leave both
       the digest and the declaration valid.

   Three states that are easily confused MUST remain distinguishable to
   a reader:

5.1.  Redaction direction

   AXES has chosen the direction for redaction after signing: salted
   per-field hash commitments, the approach established by SD-JWT
   [RFC9901].  This section records the direction and the constraints
   that led to it.  The exact construction is an open issue (Section 15)
   and nothing here is yet normative.

   Any mechanism for AXES must meet the following constraints:

   *  every reader can recompute the envelope hash, whatever they were
      shown, so the chain verifies for a redacted export;

   *  there is one digest per envelope, so chain heads and anchored
      release statements never change;

   *  preimages are byte-defined under RFC 8785, with no second
      canonicalisation;

   *  the cryptography is hash-only where possible, with a small
      verifier;

   *  a withheld field leaves a visible tombstone, distinguishable from
      a value never captured;

   *  an exporter, an auditor or an escrow can redact without the
      emitter's key;

   *  low-entropy values (amounts, booleans, short identifiers) cannot
      be guessed from their digest;




Sansone                    Expires 5 May 2027                  [Page 13]

Internet-Draft           AXES Evidence Envelope            November 2026


   *  nested objects and arrays can be redacted;

   *  unlinkability across presentations is not required, because
      evidence records are meant to link;

   *  an envelope with nothing redactable hashes exactly as it does
      today.

   Salted per-field commitments meet all of them.  Each value marked
   redactable at emission is represented inside the hashed envelope by a
   digest over a salt, the field name and the value; the salt and value
   travel separately as a disclosure.  Withholding the disclosure leaves
   the digest in place.  The envelope hash, the chain and every anchor
   therefore verify whether or not a disclosure is shown, and any holder
   of the envelope can redact by dropping a disclosure.  Fields not
   marked redactable are hashed exactly as they are now, so every digest
   published for the corpus of record stays valid.  The same approach is
   used by SD-JWT, by ISO mdoc [ISO18013-5] and by digital identity
   wallets, and has been applied to RFC 8785 JSON for agent action
   records [I-D.mih-scitt-agent-action-capsule-sel-disc].  AXES adopts
   the approach, not the SD-JWT wire format: RFC 9901 hashes the
   base64url encoding of a disclosure array, whereas an AXES disclosure
   will be defined by its RFC 8785 bytes, so that no second encoding
   enters the hash scope.  Byte compatibility with SD-JWT disclosures is
   not a goal.  A Merkle-structured envelope was considered and not
   chosen, because it changes how every envelope hash is computed and
   brings no property AXES needs that salted commitments lack.

   Disclosable values are committed with salts that travel in the
   disclosure, not with a key held by the record holder, so that any
   authorised reader can check a disclosed value without trusting
   whoever holds the data.  Stable keyed pseudonyms remain the mechanism
   for linking records about the same person (Section 17).

6.  Capture Boundary and Honest Absence

   An emitter declares a capture boundary: the kinds of action, and the
   scope, for which it commits to producing an envelope every time.
   Within a declared boundary the emitter fails closed: if it cannot
   produce the envelope, the action does not proceed past the commit
   boundary.  An action that proceeds without its envelope has left the
   declared boundary, and the emitter discloses it as soon as it can
   emit again (capture_status, Appendix C).

   The declaration is a claim by the emitter.  It does not show that the
   emitter enforced it: an emitter can bypass its own hook or act
   through a path the hook does not see.  The absence of an envelope
   within the boundary therefore supports the finding that no such



Sansone                    Expires 5 May 2027                  [Page 14]

Internet-Draft           AXES Evidence Envelope            November 2026


   action crossed the commit boundary only to the extent that
   enforcement of the boundary, over the period in question, is
   established by evidence from outside the emitter's records, for
   example counterparty acknowledgements or an independent gateway.  A
   reader MUST NOT report from absence alone that no action occurred;
   without that evidence, it reports absence as indeterminate.  Outside
   the boundary, absence means only that nothing was captured.

   When a value that a reader might expect is not present, the envelope
   SHOULD say why, by listing it in field_absences with an
   absence_reason_code.  This list is defined in the AXES vocabularies
   and is not yet exercised in the published corpus.  The reason codes
   are:

   *  not_applicable: the value does not apply to this event;

   *  not_captured: the emitter did not capture it;

   *  withheld: the value exists and is held back by the operator;

   *  redacted: the value is committed and not disclosed to this reader.

   withheld and redacted MUST NOT be read as absent.  An event outside
   the declared boundary is disclosed as outside_capture_boundary.  That
   is an honest statement of scope, not a failure.

   A reader asked whether qualifying evidence exists returns a bounded
   finding.  "No qualifying evidence was found in the declared sources
   by the stated cut-off" is a valid, verifiable finding.  It is
   different from "the sources could not be read", and both are
   different from "verified".  Registration of a record in a log, or
   anchoring of its digest, shows that the registered digest existed by
   the time of registration.  It shows that the log has not presented
   conflicting histories only where the log also supplies a consistency
   proof and the reader checks it.  It does not show that the set of
   records is complete, or that the recorded event occurred as
   described.

7.  External Anchoring

   A hash chain proves order and tamper evidence inside the emitter's
   own records.  It does not prove that the bytes existed, unmodified,
   at a time independent of whoever holds them.  An anchor provides that
   independent existence bound.

Internet-Draft           AXES Evidence Envelope            November 2026


7.1.  The anchoring block

   An anchoring block names one subject and carries one or more anchors
   over it:

   {
     "anchored_subject_type": "release_statement",
     "anchored_subject_hash": "d8b6a69f57ba2209561fb551443c096c
                               47fcb0cb6db37005fd0713690de88299",
     "anchored_subject_hash_algorithm": "SHA-256",
     "anchors": [
       {
         "anchor_id": "anch:tsa-freetsa",
         "anchoring_method": "timestamp_authority",
         "anchor_profile_ref": "axes:rfc3161_sha256_imprint@1",
         "basis_status": "demonstrated",
         "anchor_commitment_hash": "d8b6a69f...8299",
         "anchor_commitment_hash_algorithm": "SHA-256",
         "anchor_requested_at": "2026-10-12T10:12:28.000Z",
         "anchored_at": "2026-10-12T10:12:28.000Z",
         "anchor_service_id": "https://freetsa.org/tsr",
         "anchor_operator_id": "https://freetsa.org",
         "anchor_record_ref": "serial:0x09216E25",
         "anchor_proof_ref": "proofs/timestamp_authority/
                              proof_manifest.json",
         "anchor_proof_hash": "8023de42...4169",
         "anchor_verification_procedure_ref":
           "docs/anchoring-profiles.md#axesrfc3161_sha256_imprint1"
       }
     ]
   }

        Figure 1: Anchoring block over the gt-v2.1 release statement
       (abbreviated; line breaks inside strings are for presentation)

   anchored_subject_type is one of chain_head, envelope, bundle_manifest
   or release_statement.

   anchoring_method names a family of mechanisms, from an open registry
   seeded with:

   *  timestamp_authority, an RFC 3161 [RFC3161] or equivalent
      authority;

   *  opentimestamps, aggregation into a Bitcoin block [OTS];

   *  transparency_log, a leaf in an append-only verifiable log;




Sansone                    Expires 5 May 2027                  [Page 16]

Internet-Draft           AXES Evidence Envelope            November 2026


   *  distributed_ledger, a contract or registry on a ledger;

   *  write_once_store, storage that refuses overwrite and has a third-
      party verification path.

   Other mechanisms use a namespaced identifier <namespace>:<id> and
   MUST carry anchor_verification_procedure_ref.  The method value MUST
   NOT carry status; whether an anchor is real is stated only in
   basis_status.

   anchor_profile_ref names an immutable, versioned procedure that turns
   the subject digest into the value the mechanism stored
   (anchor_commitment_hash) and that says how to check it.  A changed
   procedure gets a new identifier.  A verifier recomputes the
   commitment through the profile and does not trust the stored value
   alone.

   basis_status is demonstrated (a real anchoring run produced the
   anchor), stubbed or simulated.  It is written by the producing tool
   from what the mechanism returned.

7.2.  Common verification rules

   The following rules apply to every anchor profile:

   *  Only an anchor with basis_status: demonstrated can verify.
      simulated or stubbed gives indeterminate (basis_not_demonstrated),
      never verified.

   *  A commitment that does not recompute from the subject under the
      profile, or a proof that attests a different digest, time or
      record from the one the block states, is contradicted
      (anchor_commitment_mismatch).

   *  anchor_proof_ref points to a proof manifest that lists every proof
      file with its digest, and anchor_proof_hash is the SHA-256 of the
      manifest file's bytes as stored.  A reference is resolved only
      inside the bundle that carries the block; a reference that leads
      outside it is not read.  A missing reference is indeterminate,
      attributed to the operator (anchor_proof_absent).  A reference
      that cannot be retrieved at audit time is not evaluated,
      attributed to the network (anchor_proof_unresolvable).  An altered
      proof file is contradicted (anchor_proof_hash_mismatch).

   *  An anchor that has been requested but not yet attested (it has
      anchor_requested_at and no anchored_at) is indeterminate
      (anchor_pending).




Sansone                    Expires 5 May 2027                  [Page 17]

Internet-Draft           AXES Evidence Envelope            November 2026


   *  A verifier that lacks the library, binary or procedure for a
      method MUST report not evaluated, reason instrument_failure,
      condition anchor_method_unverifiable, subject verifier.  This is
      never a pass and never a finding about the anchor.

   *  Where a block carries several anchors over one subject, each
      anchor yields its own result.  A verifier never merges them and
      never reports only the strongest.  A disagreement between anchors,
      such as times further apart than the mechanisms can explain, is
      reported, not resolved.

7.3.  Published profiles

   axes:rfc3161_sha256_imprint@1:  The subject's SHA-256 is the message
      imprint of an RFC 3161 timestamp request.  Verification checks
      that the token's messageImprint is that digest, verifies the
      token's signature against the TSA certificate chain supplied in
      the proof bundle, and checks that the token's serial equals
      anchor_record_ref and its genTime, in UTC truncated to whole
      seconds and written as .000Z, equals anchored_at.  This
      establishes that the named TSA signed the digest at the stated
      time under that chain.  Whether that chain is trusted is the
      relying party's decision.

   axes:opentimestamps_sha256_digest@1:  The subject's SHA-256 is
      submitted to OpenTimestamps calendars and, once mined, attested by
      a Bitcoin block header.  Verification replays the proof from the
      digest to the header's Merkle root and checks the header's proof
      of work against its own target. anchor_record_ref is
      block:<height>:<hash>: the height is the one stated in the proof's
      Bitcoin attestation and the hash is the header's; anchored_at
      equals the header's time.  A header does not contain its height,
      so the height, and membership of the block in Bitcoin's main
      chain, are confirmed by a separate lookup by block hash against
      any Bitcoin node.  The anchor shows that the digest existed by
      that block's time, an upper bound; it says nothing about when the
      record was first made. anchor_service_id names the chain as a
      CAIP-2 identifier [CAIP-2].

   A third profile, axes:anchor_registry_bytes32@1, covers a
   permissionless on-chain registry.  It checks offline that the
   anchoring transaction's receipt sits under the block's receipts root
   and that the block header hashes to the block hash.  Whether the
   block is on the chain's canonical history is a single lookup by block
   hash.  Such a registry has no operator: the contract has no owner,
   roles or custody, and the submitter is recorded in the event but
   holds no privilege.  For it, anchor_operator_id is null, and
   independence is a property of the chain's consensus, not of whoever



Sansone                    Expires 5 May 2027                  [Page 18]

Internet-Draft           AXES Evidence Envelope            November 2026


   submitted the anchor.  The gt-v2.1 release anchor on Base was
   submitted by a reviewer from their own wallet (Section 11).  A
   profile for transparency logs that issue COSE Receipts [RFC9942]
   under the SCITT architecture [RFC9943] is planned (Section 15).  AXES
   does not depend on SCITT: transparency_log is an anchoring method
   class, and a SCITT receipt is one way to fill it.

   The three mechanisms answer the same question with different trust.

7.4.  Earned strength

   evidence_quality.corroboration_state has an ordered set of values
   ending in externally_anchored.  That value MUST NOT be asserted
   unless at least one anchor over the subject verifies under the rules
   above.  A record that asserts externally_anchored over an anchor that
   is simulated or stubbed is contradicted
   (anchor_simulated_claims_external).  An anchor that verifies but
   whose operator is not shown to be independent of the executor and
   deployer is indeterminate (anchor_independence_unproven).  An anchor
   run by the emitter's own organisation does not show independence.




Sansone                    Expires 5 May 2027                  [Page 19]

Internet-Draft           AXES Evidence Envelope            November 2026


   Three questions are kept apart.  Whether the proof binds the digest
   is answered by the profile's verification procedure.  Whether the
   operator is independent of the executor and deployer is a separate
   result.  Whether the relying party accepts the mechanism and its
   trust chain is the relying party's decision, recorded in its
   requirement profile, and is never asserted by the record.

8.  Verification Results

   Every check a verifier or evaluator performs yields exactly one
   result, using the four-state vocabulary of
   [I-D.krausz-verification-state] under AXES key names:

   verification_state:  verified, contradicted, indeterminate or
      not_evaluated.

   verification_reason_code:  divergence (two values that should agree
      do not), absence (something needed is missing) or
      instrument_failure (the verifier could not perform the check).
      The member is absent when no reason applies, always for verified
      and for some not_evaluated results (Appendix C.1).

   verification_subject_type:  Whom the result is about: artifact (the
      record), operator (whoever produced or holds it), network
      (retrieval at audit time), verifier (the checking tool) or access
      (the reader's entitlement).

   verification_condition_code:  One value from a closed list that names
      the class of condition, for example chain_break, sequence_gap or
      anchor_pending.  The specific cause is recorded in free text
      (observed_note) and is not part of the closed vocabulary.

   The following rules apply:

   1.  A check that could not be performed is not_evaluated.  It MUST
       NOT be reported as verified, and it MUST NOT be reported as a
       finding against the record.

   2.  A check that was performed and found something missing is
       indeterminate with reason absence.  Absence is never collapsed
       into a pass.

   3.  contradicted is reserved for evidence that disagrees with itself
       or with what it asserts.

   4.  When the parties to an exchange computed different values for
       what should be the same digest, the result is indeterminate with
       reason divergence.  The verifier MUST NOT pick a side.



Sansone                    Expires 5 May 2027                  [Page 20]

Internet-Draft           AXES Evidence Envelope            November 2026


   5.  A verifier reports a result for every input it was given.  An
       input that breaks a rule of this document, such as a malformed
       timestamp, is contradicted; one the verifier lacks the capability
       to read is not_evaluated.  A verifier never stops without
       reporting.

   Rules 3 and 4 differ in what the verifier holds.  Under rule 3 it
   recomputes a value from the preimage a record states and compares it
   with the digest the same record commits to: the record contradicts
   itself.  Under rule 4 two parties supply conflicting commitments and
   nothing in the evidence gives either precedence, so the dispute stays
   open.

9.  Requirement Profiles and Evaluation

   What counts as sufficient evidence depends on who is relying on it.
   An auditor, a tax authority and a counterparty can reasonably require
   different things of the same record.  AXES therefore puts sufficiency
   outside the record.

   A requirement profile is a JSON document that names its purpose, the
   subject type it applies to, and a list of requirements.  Each
   requirement has a check type (for example field_presence,
   chain_integrity, sequence_continuity, anchoring or
   settlement_commitment) and an obligation (required, conditional,
   optional or prohibited).

   *  A profile is immutable at id@version.  Its digest is the SHA-256
      of its canonical bytes, computed by the evaluator from the bytes
      it executed.  An evaluator refuses a profile set in which two
      documents claim the same id@version with different content.

   *  Reference profiles use the axes: namespace.  Anyone MAY publish
      profiles under their own namespace.

   *  A profile MAY extend others.  Where the same requirement appears
      with different obligations, whether in a profile and one it
      extends or in two profiles it extends, the stricter wins in the
      order prohibited, required, conditional, optional, except that
      required against prohibited cannot both hold and is a conflict
      whatever the order.  A requirement marked as a floor cannot be
      relaxed by an extending profile.  A conflict, including an
      extension cycle, is indeterminate (profile_conflict), never a
      silent choice.  An extended profile that is not available is
      not_evaluated (profile_unsupported), and so is a resolved profile
      with no requirements; neither can give verified.





Sansone                    Expires 5 May 2027                  [Page 21]

Internet-Draft           AXES Evidence Envelope            November 2026


   *  The digest of a profile covers that profile only.  When it extends
      others, the evaluation record lists the digest of every profile in
      the resolved set (requirement_profile_closure), so that a reader
      who pins a profile can check which parent content was applied.

   *  A profile lists the readings it deliberately leaves unpinned.

   Evaluation applies one profile to one subject and yields an
   evaluation record.  Each requirement gives one result in the
   vocabulary of Section 8.  The overall state is:

   1.  contradicted, if any requirement is contradicted;

   2.  otherwise indeterminate, if any requirement is indeterminate or
       not evaluated;

   3.  otherwise verified.

   The overall state summarises; it does not replace.  The evaluation
   record MUST keep the result of every requirement, so that a reader
   can tell a requirement that was checked and found wanting
   (indeterminate) from one that was not checked (not_evaluated).  The
   result is described as unconditional only when every requirement is
   verified with no condition recorded.

   Obligations are applied as follows.  A field required by the profile
   and absent gives the result its declared absence reason implies, or
   indeterminate (field_absent_undeclared) when no reason is declared.
   An absent optional field does not lower the overall state; an
   optional field that is present is checked like any other, and a
   contradiction found in it counts toward the overall state.  A
   prohibited field that is absent is verified, and one that is present
   is contradicted (prohibited_field_present).  A conditional
   requirement applies only to the envelopes for which its condition
   holds.  If it holds for none, the requirement is verified with
   condition condition_not_exercised, so the guarantee is conditional: a
   safeguard that was never exercised is not reported as demonstrated.














Sansone                    Expires 5 May 2027                  [Page 22]

Internet-Draft           AXES Evidence Envelope            November 2026


   Evaluation MUST be deterministic.  It uses no model, no network
   access and no clock reading.  External facts enter only as pinned
   evidence that records when the external authority answered.  Two
   evaluators given the same subject, profile set and pinned evidence
   MUST produce the same result digest (result_core_hash).  Its preimage
   is the evaluation record without result_core_hash itself and without
   the members that identify the run rather than its findings
   (evaluation_id, evaluator_id, evaluator_version, evaluated_at and
   requirement_profile_closure), as RFC 8785 bytes; the digest is SHA-
   256.  Results appear in the order in which the profile set resolves
   (inherited requirements first), and observed_note is part of the
   preimage.

   A statement of conformance names both sides: the subject digests, the
   requirement profile with its version and digest, and the evaluation
   result.  An unqualified statement that something "conforms to AXES"
   is not meaningful under this document.

10.  Conformance Vectors

   AXES defines conformance by public, two-sided test vectors rather
   than by any implementation's behaviour.

   *  Each vector pins canonical bytes and digests, or a single expected
      result, so that implementations in different languages can compare
      byte for byte.

   *  Vectors are two-sided.  Every edge a procedure can detect has a
      vector that must pass and a vector that must fail, and each edge
      has exactly one pinned outcome.

   *  Coverage is checked: every outcome a profile or procedure declares
      must be observed in at least one vector.  A declared outcome with
      no vector is reported as a coverage failure.

   *  The reference verifier is limited to the language's standard
      library plus an RFC 8785 implementation, so that a reviewer can
      read all of it.

      of those fixes incomplete and four further defects.  The reference
      evaluator applied strictest-wins only between a profile and its
      own requirements, not between two profiles it extends.  The
      reference hash helper excluded both signature member names under
      either release.  The SDK could evaluate envelopes other than the
      bytes whose digest it recorded, and its manifest path and some
      input limits were not enforced.  Each finding from both runs is
      fixed, with a regression test, and the rules they touched are
      stated in Section 4, Section 4.2, Section 7 and Section 9.  No
      published digest or evaluation record changed.  Two design points
      it raised remain open: pinning the key-alias table alongside the
      profiles, and bounding the work of resolving a large profile
      graph.

   Python SDK:  axes-sdk 0.1.1 [AXES-SDK] packages strict ingestion,
      stream and bundle verification, deterministic profile evaluation
      and a command-line tool for use outside the repository.  Its tests
      reproduce the reference results on the corpus of record, the
      published evaluation records and the profile vectors.  It is
      written by the authors from the reference tooling, so it is not an
      independent implementation.

   Disclosed limitations of the corpus:  Envelope signatures in the
      corpus are stubs and are marked envelope_signature_basis_status:
      stubbed.  In-trace anchors are simulated and marked basis_status:
      simulated.  The reference verifier reports them as indeterminate
      (basis_not_demonstrated), and no envelope reports contradicted.

13.  Binding to Carrying Protocols

   This section is informative.  It describes the direction for binding
   an AXES record to the protocol that carried the action (item 8 in
   Section 15).

   An AXES record sits beside the carrying protocol and binds to that
   protocol's artefacts through a named, versioned and immutable binding
   profile, identified as <namespace>:<lower_snake_name>@<version> in
   the same way as anchor and requirement profiles.  A binding profile
   declares:

   *  the protocol and the version of it that it binds;

   *  the protocol artefacts it references, each hashed exactly as
      received and never re-serialised;

   *  for each field it uses, whether the field is never redactable,
      disclosable by role (committed with a salted commitment,
      Section 5.1), or kept out of the record and referenced by digest;



Sansone                    Expires 5 May 2027                  [Page 27]

Internet-Draft           AXES Evidence Envelope            November 2026


   *  a verification procedure with two-sided vectors (Section 10).

   Binding profiles add or tighten requirements.  They never relax a
   core rule and never make a never-redactable field redactable.  A
   record may carry several bindings, for example a payment binding and
   an authorisation binding for the same action.  A reader that does not
   implement a profile reports the binding as not evaluated
   (profile_unsupported), never as verified.  Protocol owners may
   publish binding profiles under their own namespace.

   AXES separates three layers: the evidence record (layer 1), the
   binding of a record to the carrying protocol (layer 2) and an
   existence anchor (layer 3, Section 7).  A carrying protocol needs
   only layer 2: a digest, an algorithm label, a record type and a join
   key.

   The first binding is to x402 [X402-TSC4].  The published correlation
   record (axes:x402_correlation@1) binds evidence to an x402 payment by
   digest and is checked against public vectors from the x402 community.
   A profile for the x402 payment anchor, including the salted
   authorisation commitment proposed for x402 receipts [PGC-17], is in
   preparation.  Several agent payment-mandate and intent protocols
   carry SD-JWT credentials.  Because AXES commitments follow the same
   approach (Section 5.1), a binding can reference such a disclosure by
   its digest, as received, without translating it.  Identifiers for
   further bindings are reserved in the binding profile catalogue of
   [AXES-REPO].

14.  Operational Considerations

   Key custody and rotation:  Envelope signatures are not yet specified
      (Section 15), but every envelope already carries
      integrity.signing_key_id inside its hash scope.  Signing keys
      SHOULD be held outside the emitter's application process, for
      example in a hardware or managed key service, and each key's
      provenance SHOULD be referenced
      (integrity.signing_key_provenance_ref).  Rotation creates a new
      key identifier and never invalidates records made under an earlier
      key.  The same rule applies to salt sources and pseudonym keys.

   Anchoring cadence:  Until a stream is first anchored externally, its
      emitter can rewrite it wholesale.  After that, it can rewrite only
      the envelopes emitted since the latest anchor, because anchoring a
      chain head commits every earlier envelope.  The interval between
      anchors is therefore the window in which rewriting cannot be
      detected.  Deployers SHOULD declare the interval, choose it from
      what their relying parties need, and anchor at every commit
      boundary of high consequence.



Sansone                    Expires 5 May 2027                  [Page 28]

Internet-Draft           AXES Evidence Envelope            November 2026


   Omission:  Once envelope signatures are specified, sequence_number
      and integrity.previous_envelope_hash sit inside a signed payload.
      An omitted envelope then shows as a sequence gap or a chain break,
      and repairing it means re-signing the stream, which contradicts
      any chain head already anchored.  Omission becomes detectable in
      practice only together with heartbeats at a declared interval, an
      explicit close of each stream that states its final sequence
      number, regular anchoring and, where possible, a counterparty that
      records its own side of the exchange.  None of these detects an
      action that the emitter never captured (Section 6).

   Redaction:  When the construction is specified (Section 5.1), each
      salt is expected to carry at least 128 bits from a
      cryptographically secure random source and to be used for one
      commitment only.  Fields must be marked redactable when the
      envelope is emitted.  The number of commitments in an envelope is
      visible unless decoy digests are added.  Disclosures, with their
      salts, must be kept for as long as the envelope may be relied on;
      destroying them is how a value is erased (Section 17).

   Retention:  Envelopes are kept for at least as long as any record in
      the stream may be relied on, which for financial records can be
      longer than the limitation period of the obligation they evidence.
      Clear values are never kept longer than the envelopes that commit
      to them.  Retention classes, production on demand and successor
      custodians are open issues.

   Offline verification:  The reference verifier and anchor verifier run
      offline from a bundle.  A check that needs the network, such as
      whether a block is on a chain's canonical history or whether a
      proof can be retrieved, is reported as not evaluated when the
      network is unavailable, never as verified.

15.  Open Issues

   The following are known gaps.  Reviews and counter-proposals are
   welcome.

   1.   Redaction-tolerant hashing.  The intended direction is salted
        per-field hash commitments, the approach of SD-JWT [RFC9901]
        with an RFC 8785 disclosure encoding (Section 5.1).  Each value
        marked redactable at emission is represented inside the hashed
        envelope by a digest over a salt, the field name and the value;
        the salt and value travel separately as a disclosure.  The
        envelope hash, the chain and every anchor then verify whether or
        not a disclosure is shown, an envelope with no redactable field
        hashes exactly as it does today, and any holder can redact
        without the emitter's key.  Open: the byte form of the



Sansone                    Expires 5 May 2027                  [Page 29]

Internet-Draft           AXES Evidence Envelope            November 2026


        disclosure under RFC 8785, salt length and encoding, nested
        objects and array elements, decoy digests, and which fields each
        module marks redactable.  Before the construction is normative
        it needs two-sided vectors for nested objects, arrays, duplicate
        disclosures, missing and altered salts, a disclosure substituted
        from another envelope, and low-entropy values.  This gates the
        envelope schema.

   2.   The full envelope JSON Schema and field catalogue (two of
        sixteen planned modules are published for challenge).  Key names
        in the published corpus and in the catalogue have not yet been
        reconciled in every case.

   3.   The extension model: how a reader processes an extension it does
        not understand (must_understand, unknown_field_policy).
        Extensions are already inside the hash scope.

   4.   The envelope signature profile over the canonical bytes (JWS or
        COSE).  The corpus signatures are stubs.

   5.   The amendment model for superseding envelopes.

   6.   Whether recorded_at, which is written by the recipient, should
        be authenticated by a countersignature from the recipient rather
        than sit in the emitter's hash scope.  It is in scope today; any
        change would land only in an announced release.

   7.   An anchor profile for transparency logs (COSE Receipts with
        RFC9162_SHA256).  A profile for a permissionless on-chain
        registry exists in the reference tooling.

   8.   The binding of evidence to payment and authorisation protocols,
        x402 first, through named, versioned binding profiles.

   9.   Registration procedures for the AXES vocabularies (see
        Section 18).

   10.  Retention and obtainability: who holds a record, for how long
        relative to limitation periods, how it is produced on demand
        years later, and what happens when the holder ceases to exist.

   11.  An evidence bundle for disputes: a registered media type and a
        manifest of included artefacts with digests.  It would also
        carry the set of verification results, including what was not
        checked, and an explicit statement of what the bundle does not
        contain.





Sansone                    Expires 5 May 2027                  [Page 30]

Internet-Draft           AXES Evidence Envelope            November 2026


   12.  Binding accountability to a legal person, rather than only to a
        key: the identifier scheme, the basis of the binding and how it
        was verified.

   13.  Binding the terms of an offer (price, asset, payee) into the
        record of the action that settles against it, and recording
        where what settled differs from what was offered.

   14.  Digest algorithm migration: how a long-lived stream, its anchors
        and its evaluation records move to a new digest algorithm while
        earlier digests stay verifiable.

16.  Security Considerations

   Three properties are distinct, and none implies another:

   *  Integrity: the bytes a reader holds agree with the committed
      digest.  This document specifies it.

   *  Authenticity: a named identity or key produced or endorsed those
      bytes.  It needs the envelope signature profile, which is open
      (Section 15); the corpus signatures are stubs.  Anchoring the
      digest of a fabricated record does not authenticate its claims.

   *  Completeness: the relevant population of actions is represented.
      It is bounded by Section 6 and is never shown by integrity or
      anchoring alone.

   No statement made under this document implies all three.

   The emitter may be the adversary:  An envelope records what was
      claimed, by whom, under which authority and with what
      corroboration.  It does not prove that the agent or the emitter
      behaved honestly.  Self-declared values are not authenticated by
      being in the hash scope.  Fields that record independence or
      custody MUST NOT be read as established by the record itself.

      In particular, the authority fields are claims that an authority
      existed and was relied on.  Verifying that authority needs a
      grant, credential or policy version that can be checked outside
      the record, with its scope and validity at the commit boundary.
      Binding authority to a legal person is open issue 12.

   Scope narrower than consumption:  If a reader consumes a field that







Sansone                    Expires 5 May 2027                  [Page 31]

Internet-Draft           AXES Evidence Envelope            November 2026


      the digest does not cover, that field can be altered while the
      record verifies.  Section 5 makes inclusion the default, requires
      a reason for every exclusion, and puts the scope declaration
      inside its own scope.  Implementers MUST NOT read fields from
      outside the declared hash scope as authenticated.

   Canonicalisation differentials:  Two verifiers that accept different
      inputs, or that canonicalise the same input differently, can reach
      different verdicts on the same bytes.  Section 4 therefore
      requires I-JSON input, sorting by UTF-16 code units and no Unicode
      normalisation, and Section 4.2 forbids fractional numbers and
      integers outside the exactly representable range.  A general-
      purpose RFC 8785 library may accept such values silently; an AXES
      verifier checks the domain before it canonicalises.

   Digest labels:  Because the digest algorithm is agile, a digest is
      meaningful only together with its algorithm label.  A verifier
      MUST NOT compare digests whose algorithm labels differ.

   Simulated or self-held anchors:  A simulated anchor, or one held by
      the emitter's own organisation, gives no independent existence
      bound.  Section 7.4 makes the strong claim depend on verification.
      Relying parties decide whether to trust a timestamp authority's
      certificate chain.  A Bitcoin header's proof of work does not by
      itself show main-chain membership.

   Registration is not completeness:  An inclusion proof, a time stamp
      or a ledger record shows that a digest was committed.  It does not
      show that every relevant record was committed, or that the
      recorded event occurred as described.  Readers MUST NOT report
      completeness from anchoring alone.

   Absence:  An attacker who can suppress envelopes can make evidence
      absent.  Sequence continuity, heartbeats, fail-closed emission
      within a declared boundary and the distinction between withheld,
      redacted and never captured exist so that suppression is visible
      as indeterminate rather than invisible.

   What this specification cannot detect:  This section states the
      limits, so that readers infer no more than a record supports.

      *  An emitter that controls capture can omit actions outside its
         declared capture boundary, and nothing in the record shows
         them.







Sansone                    Expires 5 May 2027                  [Page 32]

Internet-Draft           AXES Evidence Envelope            November 2026


      *  Until envelope signatures are specified (Section 15), an
         emitter can rewrite its own stream wholesale before the stream
         is first anchored externally.  Anchoring bounds that window to
         the interval between anchors.

      *  A completeness or count field computed by the party that holds
         the record is a claim by that party, not evidence of
         completeness.

      *  Registration in a log or on a ledger shows that what was
         registered existed by that time.  It shows that the log
         presented one history only with a consistency proof that the
         reader checks, and never that every relevant record was
         registered.

17.  Privacy Considerations

   Envelopes carry references and digests, not payloads.  People
   (approvers, operators, customers, employees) are referenced by
   pseudonymous keys and are never embedded.  Erasure is supported by
   destroying referenced content, so the envelope keeps its digest while
   the content is gone.  A reader should report such content as erased
   rather than missing; that reporting outcome will be registered with
   the redaction construction (Section 15).

   A digest of a low-entropy value can be reversed by guessing.
   Anchoring a digest publishes it permanently.  Emitters SHOULD anchor
   only digests of high-entropy subjects (chain heads, bundle manifests,
   release statements) or salted commitments, and SHOULD NOT anchor
   digests of individual values that identify a person.

   Identifiers scoped to one relying party deliberately produce
   different digests at different relying parties, which limits
   correlation of the same person across relying parties.

   These measures reduce exposure.  They do not by themselves make a
   record anonymous, establish compliance with any data-protection law,
   or make erasure effective in every legal setting.  Pseudonymous
   identifiers, digests and metadata such as timing, counts and sequence
   can still allow a person to be re-identified or records to be linked,
   and a salted commitment protects the committed value from guessing,
   not the metadata around it.

   Redaction after signing will use salted per-field commitments
   (Section 5.1); the construction is not yet specified (Section 15).
   Until it is, sensitive values should be kept out of envelopes by
   reference rather than excluded from the hash.




Sansone                    Expires 5 May 2027                  [Page 33]

Internet-Draft           AXES Evidence Envelope            November 2026


   The intended direction for sensitive data separates two planes.  The
   chain plane holds envelopes: hashed, chained, anchored and never
   erased, and it never holds personal data in clear.  A raw plane holds
   clear values, salts and referenced artefacts under a named custodian
   whose relationship to the emitter and deployer is recorded.  Erasing
   a data subject's values destroys their raw-plane disclosures and
   keys: the commitments remain in the chain plane, cannot be reversed
   by guessing because they are salted, and the value is reported as
   erased rather than missing.  Each re-disclosure of raw-plane values
   is itself recorded as evidence.  These are proposed decisions in the
   AXES decision register [AXES-REPO] and are not specified by this
   document.

A.3.  Digest and chain link

   The SHA-256 of these bytes, as bare lowercase hexadecimal, is

   741e26e97a1e375f4eb1f007ab98ead5fd5bfb4e5c28092649c90acde6f38ab8

   which equals the stored integrity.envelope_hash.  The stored
   integrity.previous_envelope_hash,

   52c2bfef03ba11c71b906b17e9105f3623c9918687f5eb7c6e218a670495a9f9

   is the recomputed envelope hash of envelope 8, and envelope 10
   carries the digest above as its own integrity.previous_envelope_hash.
   Sequence numbers 8, 9 and 10 are contiguous.  Continuing the same
   computation to envelope 76 gives the financial chain head stated in
   Section 11.

   The integrity.envelope_signature value is a stub
   (envelope_signature_basis_status: stubbed) and lies outside the hash
   scope, so it does not affect the digest.  Once redaction is specified
   (Section 5.1), this envelope, which has no redactable field, keeps
   exactly this digest.

A.4.  A changed value

   Suppose a holder changes the authority this envelope relied on,
   replacing "authority_context_ref":"AD-7844" with
   "authority_context_ref":"AD-7845" (line 8 of the canonical bytes
   above).  The byte count stays 2130, and the SHA-256 becomes

   56baaf278e977fc76e3b52242bc4fe0c8d24c18bf467a5d472af3b1c93d36d17

   which differs from the stored integrity.envelope_hash.  A verifier
   reports contradicted, reason divergence, condition
   envelope_hash_mismatch, subject artifact (Section 4.4).  If the
   holder also overwrites the stored hash with the new digest, envelope
   10's integrity.previous_envelope_hash no longer matches it and the
   verifier reports chain_break.  Hiding the change would mean rewriting
   every later envelope, which changes the chain head, and the chain
   head and release statement digests are anchored outside the emitter
   (Section 7).

Appendix B.  Data Definitions in CDDL

   This appendix gives the settled structures of this document in CDDL
   [RFC8610], applied to JSON.  It is informative and checks shape only.
   The normative rules are in the sections named in each comment, and
   the conditions CDDL does not express are checked by those sections'
   procedures: that a digest has the length of its algorithm (64
   hexadecimal characters for SHA-256), that the algorithm and
   canonicalisation identifier are supported, that a timestamp is a real
   instant, that a condition code is in the closed list of Appendix C.1,
   and that an anchor without anchored_at is pending.  The envelope as a
   whole is not defined here, because its schema is an open issue
   (Section 15).  The closed list of condition codes is in Appendix C.1.

   These definitions accept every integrity block (152) and every
   anchoring block in the corpus of record, every release anchoring
   block and anchor verification result in [AXES-REPO], and every
   published evaluation record.  Each rule also rejects a copy of a real
   instance with one value broken. ietf/check_appendices.py in
   [AXES-REPO] runs these checks.

   The following vocabularies are used by this document.  Values are
   lower_snake.  Every list except anchoring_method is closed: a new
   value is added only in an announced release, and a value is never
   redefined or removed.  Each list is maintained in [AXES-REPO]
   (Section 18).

   verification_state:  verified, contradicted, indeterminate,
      not_evaluated (Section 8).

   verification_reason_code:  divergence, absence, instrument_failure.

   verification_subject_type:  artifact, operator, network, verifier,
      access.

   absence_reason_code:  not_applicable, not_captured, withheld,
      redacted (Section 6).
